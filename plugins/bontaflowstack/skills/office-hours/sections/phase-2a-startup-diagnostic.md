# Startup diagnosis

Use this reference when the goal is a business or internal operational outcome.
Follow HOST; this is a question bank, not six mandatory interrogation rounds.
Read existing evidence first and ask the smallest useful unanswered question.

| Topic | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Demand | Actual use, payment, reliance or consequences of losing the solution; distinguish these from compliments and signups. |
| Status quo | The present workaround, its cost and why the person continues using it. |
| Specific user | The role and concrete task; a real example where available. Personal names are unnecessary. |
| Narrow wedge | The smallest useful deliverable someone will use or an internal sponsor can evaluate. |
| Observation | What users actually do, where they get stuck, and the gap between their words and the product pitch. |
| Future fit | What could make the solution more valuable or obsolete; label an unverified thesis as an assumption. |

Before a product exists, concentrate on demand, workaround and user. With users,
inspect observed behavior and the narrow improvement. With paying users, inspect
retention/value and future fit. For internal infrastructure, adapt value and
success to its team and sponsor instead of inventing commercial demand.

Be direct and evidence-based, without forced discomfort or repeated pressure.
State the strongest plausible interpretation, the missing evidence and what
would change the conclusion. Respect the user's decision to change modes or
stop diagnosis. Finish with one concrete validation action or a supported design.
